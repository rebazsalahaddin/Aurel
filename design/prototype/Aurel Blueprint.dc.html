Aurel — Design Refinement Blueprint iPhone · A1–C1 English
Senior design review against the English Learning App Refinement Specification. Internal working document.

Product design blueprint

Aurel: what to keep, what to change, and what to build next.

This is a diagnosis of the existing 18-screen Aurel prototype, followed by a complete redesign plan. It is written to be built: every recommendation names the screen it touches, the reason it exists, and its priority. Nothing here proposes a rebuild — Aurel's identity, tone, and learning model are its strongest assets and are preserved throughout.

Scope note

The prototype was inspected screen by screen and state by state before any recommendation was written. Full 21-field specifications are given for the eight screens that change materially; the remaining ten are covered in a change matrix so the document stays usable. Anything the current build does not answer is listed under Needs Product Clarification rather than invented.

1 · Executive design diagnosis

Aurel already looks like a premium product and already sounds like an intelligent one. Its problem is not aesthetic and not tonal — it is structural: the app is a beautifully dressed library of screens rather than a daily practice. Three specific consequences:

There is no shape to a day. The home screen shows a chapter path and a streak counter, but never states what today asks of the learner or when today is finished. Nothing anywhere says "you are done." A streak with no defined unit of completion is a number without a rule, and the spec's daily loop (Loop 3) has no surface at all.

Progress measures effort, not ability. Words retained, minutes, lessons — all inputs. The learner cannot see whether their listening is stronger than their grammar, what "mastered" means, or what they can now do in the world. The ladder on Progress is the right metaphor but is currently a static list of five CEFR rows with no evidence behind the labels.

The differentiators are unfinished. Speaking and conversation are the two areas where a warm, unhurried English app can beat the market outright. Today speaking is one prompt, a static waveform, and an undefined score of "Clear"; conversation as a lesson type does not exist. The most competitive part of the product is the least built.

The fix is not more screens. It is one added spine — a defined day with two short returns — plus finishing the two flows that make Aurel unlike anything else. Everything in this document serves that.

2 · Current strengths — protect these

1. A genuine visual identity. The dawn-horizon welcome, terracotta-and-sage palette, Caprasimo headings, contour ambient layer, and the A-with-horizon wordmark make Aurel recognisable from a single screenshot without its logo. This satisfies the hardest requirement in the specification and must not be diluted.

2. Tone with no shame in it. "Loose ends" for the review queue. "A start is a start." Two rest days a month built in. "No prizes" under the leaderboard. This is the anti-toxicity requirement already solved, and it is a real brand asset — most competitors cannot say any of it credibly.

3. The placement review screen. "Nothing is recorded until you confirm" with every answer editable before commitment is a trust pattern almost nobody in this category has. Extend the principle; never remove it.

4. One action per screen. Every screen has exactly one dominant button. The home path has exactly one open node. The "one next action" principle is already met.

5. Question variety and a coherent motion language. Five interaction types ship already (card, choice, pairs, listening, word order), with per-question re-entry animation, answer feedback, count-ups, and a reduced-motion path. Motion is purposeful rather than decorative.

6. Restraint in monetization. Two plans, four honest feature lines, a visible cancel path in Settings. No countdown, no scarcity. Keep exactly as is.

4 · Design opportunities

Four openings, in order of leverage. Each is expanded later.

A. Own the day, not the session. Aurel's promise is "one short lesson a day". Nothing in the interface enacts it. Giving the day a visible shape — and a visible end — converts the brand line into a mechanic (§23, §42).

B. Make the horizon the instrument. The dawn imagery is currently decoration on two screens. Turned into the progress display, it becomes the visual signature the specification asks for and replaces a generic progress bar with something no competitor has.

C. Sell capability, not points. "You can now introduce yourself to a stranger at work" is both the honest description of a finished chapter and the most motivating sentence the app could print. It costs one content field per chapter.

D. Speak without scoring theatre. Competitors fake pronunciation precision. Aurel's tone allows it to be the app that says "here is your voice next to a native's; you decide" — with a confidence-graded verdict only where recognition is reliable. Trust becomes the feature.

5 · Information architecture

The existing four destinations — Learn, Practice, Progress, You — are correct and validated against the build. Do not adopt a five-tab structure; Compete does not deserve a tab in a product whose leaderboard is deliberately low-stakes. It stays where it is: a row inside You and a card inside Progress.

Two IA corrections:

Review must be reachable from Learn. Today "Loose ends" lives only under Practice. Spaced review is the engine of the product's learning claim and is currently two taps from the home screen. It belongs on Home as the day's second act.

Stories need a reader. The Stories list navigates nowhere. Either build the reader (P2) or remove the list until it exists — a dead-end list on a premium product reads as a broken build, and three items where two are locked reads as an empty shelf.

Final structure

Learn — today's arc, the chapter path, review entry. · Practice — speaking, conversation scenarios, review, stories. · Progress — skills, ladder, history, group. · You — identity, milestones, subscription, settings.

6 · Navigation proposal

Keep the floating four-item glass tab bar; it is native in feel and reads as a control layer rather than content, which is the correct use of a glass material. Three refinements:

Lessons stay modal. The tab bar already hides during a lesson — correct. Add an interrupted-lesson resume prompt when the learner leaves mid-lesson (§57): "Pick up question 3 of 5?" on the next launch.

Add one badge, never more. A single dot on Learn when the day's arc is unfinished. No counts, no red, no badge on any other tab.

Back is always a back-chevron in the same position. The build is consistent here (44×44, top-left, hairline ring) with one exception: the paywall uses a top-right close and the lesson a top-left close. Both are correct for modals — document the distinction rather than unifying it.

7 · Home screen redesign

The current home is a header, a five-node path, an unlock note, and a next-chapter card. It answers "where am I in the curriculum" but not "what does today ask of me". The redesign inserts one module above the path and changes nothing else about the path's geometry.

Recommendation R1 · Today's arc

What: Replace the streak pill with a full-width horizon module directly under the chapter title. It contains the day's two acts — Dawn (the lesson, 6 min) and Sundown (recall of items due today, 60 sec) — drawn as a sun travelling an arc over the dune silhouette already used on the welcome screen. The sun sits at the left rim before the lesson, at the meridian after it, and sets when recall is done. The arc completes; the streak counts completed arcs.

Why: the daily loop has no surface today. · UX: gives the home screen a single unambiguous next action and, for the first time, a defined end. · Learning: the second act is the spaced-repetition queue, so the habit and the retention mechanism are the same gesture. · Retention: two short returns a day beats one long one, and a half-finished arc is a stronger pull than a number. · Visual: promotes the brand's dawn motif from decoration to instrument. · Complexity: medium — one new component, one scheduler query, no new data model. · Priority: P0.

Hierarchy, top to bottom: chapter kicker and name (unchanged) → today's arc with its one CTA → the chapter path → next chapter. The streak moves inside the arc module as a quiet numeral with its rule stated once ("complete both acts"). The unlock note is absorbed into the locked nodes themselves (R6).

What not to change: the dotted path geometry, the single open node with its breathing halo, the sage completion nodes, the header gradient. They work.

8 · Learning journey

The chapter path is the right primary metaphor — keep it as the only one. The ladder on Progress is its long-range view, not a second metaphor, and should be drawn as the same dotted line at a smaller scale so the relationship reads.

Add a chapter opening and a chapter close. Twenty-four chapters with no ceremony between them wastes the strongest emotional beat in the curriculum. Opening: one full-bleed screen naming the chapter and what it will make possible ("By the end: you can hold a first conversation with a stranger at work"). Close: the same sentence in past tense, plus the two or three items that are still weak and will be carried forward. P1.

Name the levels in the learner's language. A2 · Elementary is accurate and cold. Keep the CEFR band as the small technical label and give each level a plain name in the same slot the chapter uses: Foundation, Everyday, Confident, Fluent, Precise. P2.

9 · Lesson interaction system

Five types exist; the target is eight, with a rhythm rule rather than a random shuffle.

Type State Action
Card revealShips; 3D flip is goodAdd "knew it / didn't" self-rating to feed the scheduler
Multiple choiceShipsAdd second-attempt path (R4)
PairsShips, tap-basedDrag with tap fallback; add a check glyph to matched tiles
ListeningShips; waveform is decorativeReal amplitude, 0.7× replay, unlimited replays without penalty
Word orderShips, tap-basedDrag with tap fallback; keep the dashed tray
Say itNewOne sentence, spoken; the speaking flow inside a lesson (P0)
Which is naturalNewTwo correct sentences, one idiomatic — the register lesson (P1)
Scenario replyNewChoose a reply inside a conversation; the answer changes what comes next (P1)

Rhythm rule for a 5–7 item lesson: introduce (card) → recognise (choice or pairs) → hear (listening) → produce (order or say-it) → apply (scenario). Never two of the same type adjacent; always finish on production, never on recognition. This is what makes a lesson feel like it goes somewhere.

Keep: the segmented progress bar, the sticky glass footer with a single primary button, the per-question re-entry animation, the kicker naming the skill being practised. All correct.

Recommendation R4 · Second attempt before the answer

What: On a first wrong answer to a choice, listening, or order item, do not reveal the answer. Show a one-line nudge naming the class of error ("The verb needs to agree with she") and re-enable the options with the chosen one dimmed. Reveal fully on the second miss, with the current explanation. Mark the item for review either way; count it correct-on-retry in analytics, not in the score.

Why: the learner currently never re-performs the item. · UX: converts a dead-end into an action. · Learning: retrieval after a hint is the highest-value moment in the whole session; giving the answer away spends it. · Retention: removes the small sting of being told you were wrong with nothing to do. · Visual: reuses the existing verdict slot — no new component. · Complexity: low. · Priority: P0.

10 · Vocabulary

The flashcard is well made but thin: front, back, one example. A word should arrive with everything needed to use it. Target card back: meaning, audio (tap the word to hear it), one example in the learner's stated goal context, one collocation, and a register note where it matters ("Farewell — formal, rare in speech").

Add a word detail sheet reachable from any word in a lesson or the review list — the same content as a native sheet, with a mastery state (see §17) and "seen in 4 lessons". This is the vocabulary product without building a dictionary screen. P2.

11 · Grammar

The build already avoids textbook framing — grammar appears as "register", "word order", "contractions" inside lessons, which is right. Strengthen with pattern discovery: before the rule, show three correct examples and ask what changed. Reveal the rule as a single sentence afterwards, never before. Add a before/after comparison to the verdict for structure items: the learner's sentence above, the natural one below, with the single differing span tinted. P1.

12 · Listening

Three changes to the existing screen. Real waveform driven by the audio buffer's amplitude, not the current animated placeholder — a fake waveform on a premium product is the kind of detail reviewers notice. 0.7× replay as a secondary control beside the play button, labelled "Slower", with no penalty and no limit. Reconstruct mode as a second listening type: hear the sentence, then build it from a word bank (reusing the order component).

Every audio item needs a transcript reveal after the answer, and an audio-failure state: "Audio didn't load — try again, or skip this one." P0 for the failure state, P1 for the rest.

13 · Speaking

Recommendation R2 · The five-step speaking loop

What: Rebuild the Speak screen as a repeatable loop: 1 Hear the native line (waveform A) → 2 Say it (live waveform, hold or tap) → 3 Compare your recording against the native one, two stacked waveforms, both replayable → 4 Read a verdict that names at most two specific words, never a percentage → 5 Again, with the previous attempt kept visible so improvement is felt. Free conversation stays, as a separate mode below the loop.

Why: the current screen records and grades with nothing to compare against. · UX: gives the microphone a purpose beyond a single take. · Learning: hearing your own voice next to a model is the mechanism by which pronunciation actually improves. · Retention: "again" is the most natural just-one-more in the product. · Visual: two waveforms in the accent and sage roles is a strong, ownable image. · Complexity: medium-high — AVFoundation record/playback plus real amplitude metering; the verdict copy is content work. · Priority: P0.

On scoring honesty. Never show a number. Use three verdicts, each requiring a stated confidence threshold from the recogniser: Clear, Nearly — one word to fix, Hard to make out. Below threshold, say so: "I couldn't hear that well enough to judge — try once more?" This is both the accessible behaviour and the trustworthy one.

Required states: permission not yet asked, permission denied (with the Settings deep link), no input detected, too loud, offline (on-device recognition only), and a "listening" state that visibly responds to the room. The mic pulse rings that exist today are good — keep them, and make the waveform live. Every speaking item needs a skip that costs nothing.

14 · Conversation

This is the product's biggest unbuilt opportunity. Build Scenes: eight to twelve short scenarios per level, each three to five turns, tied to the goals already collected in onboarding (work, travel, exam, self). A scene is a bubble thread reusing the speak screen's existing bubble styles.

The turn: the other person speaks (text plus audio) → the learner picks one of three replies, all grammatical, differing in register → the scene continues differently depending on the choice → a one-line note explains the register difference without marking anyone wrong ("That works. A shade blunt for a first meeting — Yes, please lands softer.").

The close: a capability statement — "You can order a coffee and handle a follow-up question" — plus the option to replay the scene speaking your own replies aloud (which merges scenes with R2 and is where the product becomes genuinely distinctive).

Scenes live in Practice as the first card, above speaking. Priority P1; the single highest-value P1 in this document.

15 · Review system

"Loose ends" has the best copy in the app and the wrong behaviour: its button restarts the whole lesson. Make it run a session of exactly the due items, in the interaction type they were originally missed in, three to eight items, 60–90 seconds. This session is the Sundown act on Home (R1).

Show the interval honestly and simply. Keep "Due tomorrow / in 2 days / in 4 days". Add one line per item on success — "Back in 8 days" — so the widening interval is felt rather than explained. Never expose the algorithm.

Empty state: the existing "Nothing loose" is good. Add the productive next step ("Nothing due until tomorrow — a scene, instead?") so the empty state is never a dead end.

16 · Progress system

Recommendation R3 · Five skills, not one score

What: Replace the top of the Progress screen with five horizontal skill meters — Vocabulary, Grammar, Listening, Speaking, Conversation — each showing a mastery state (§17) rather than a percentage, sorted with the weakest first and labelled "Weakest — worth ten minutes". Keep the minutes chart and the ladder below. Do not invent a single composite "English score"; the methodology does not support one.

Why: the learner cannot currently see what they are becoming better at. · UX: answers the question the screen's title promises. · Learning: surfacing the weakest skill directs practice where it pays, and makes adaptive difficulty legible. · Retention: a nearly-full meter is a stronger reason to return than a streak number. · Visual: five bars in one accent, sorted — calm, and it fills the screen's current emptiness with substance instead of cards. · Complexity: medium; needs per-item skill tagging, which the lesson data already implies via its kickers. · Priority: P0.

Also: the 8-week minutes chart measures attendance, not ability — relabel it "Time practised" and demote it below the skills. The empty-state ghost bars with "Eight weeks from now this will say something" are excellent; keep that exact copy.

17 · Mastery

Define five states and use them everywhere a word, structure, or skill appears — the same five words, in the same order, with the same shapes:

State Earned by Cue (never colour alone)
SeenShown onceHairline ring, no fill
PractisedOne correct answerQuarter-filled ring
FamiliarCorrect across two sessionsHalf-filled ring
StrongCorrect after a 7-day gapFull ring
MasteredCorrect after a 30-day gap, and produced (spoken or built) at least onceFull ring with a sage check

Note the requirement in the last row: mastery requires production, not lesson completion. This is what makes the word honest, and it is the difference between Aurel's claim and a completion bar. P0 for the definition, P1 for the rings.

18 · Streak

The Streak screen is already the healthiest in the category — 96px numeral, week dots, month grid, and "Two rest days a month are built in." Three additions, no removals: state the rule inside the screen ("A day counts when both acts of the arc are done"), show best streak beside current, and make the rest days visible as tokens — two small sage dots, spent or unspent, so the grace mechanism is a resource the learner can see rather than a promise in a paragraph.

On a miss: never a broken-streak screen. The month grid simply shows a hollow day, and the home arc reads "Yesterday stayed unfinished. Today is open." No red, no flame, no repair purchase. P1.

19 · Reward economy

Aurel currently has no currency at all — which is a defensible position and better than five. Recommendation: one progression measure, no coins, no gems, no shop.

Use words retained — already the number on the leaderboard and Progress. It is legible, it is honest, and unlike XP it cannot be farmed by re-running easy lessons because retention is measured after a gap. Do not add XP on top of it; two numbers would make both meaningless.

Secondary rewards, deliberately few: milestone statements (§22), unlockable stories, and one cosmetic — a sky for the home horizon, earned at 30, 100, and 365 completed arcs (dawn, high summer, winter dusk). Cosmetic reward that reinforces the signature visual rather than competing with it. P2.

20 · Competition

Keep Cedar Group — a 30-person weekly cohort with no prizes is the right amount of competition for this brand. Fix its actionability, per the specification's own example:

Lead with the narrative, not the table. Above the rows: "You're 6th · 12 words to 5th · 2 days left." Then show only rank 1–3, the two rows either side of the learner, and the learner's own row pinned — not eight arbitrary rows. Tapping "show all" expands.

Bring the rows into the material system. They are currently the only list in the app with transparent backgrounds — glass rows, with the learner's row accent-tinted as elsewhere.

Rules and exit. One tappable "How this works" line ("Words retained this week. Cohorts are matched on activity. Nobody is removed.") and an opt-out in Settings that hides the tab row and the Progress card. No demotion, no relegation, no last-place styling. P2.

21 · Social

Nothing social exists today, and that is fine for the MVP. If it is built, the order is: 1 invite a friend into your cohort (P3), 2 a two-person scene where each takes a role (P3, and the most on-brand social idea available), 3 nothing else. No feeds, no reactions, no follower counts. Privacy defaults: display name only, no location, cohort visibility opt-in — note that the current leaderboard shows city names ("Lisbon", "Chengdu"), which would need consent.

22 · Achievements

Do not add badges. Add milestones — a sentence and a date, not an icon grid, shown in the You screen as a short list. Twelve for the first year, not forty.

Each is a capability or a real quantity: First conversation held. · 100 words strong. · Seven days, unbroken. · First scene without a hint. · 50 sentences spoken aloud. · A story finished without the transcript. · Restaurant English, working.

Celebration: two seconds, in place, no full-screen takeover — the row writes itself in with the existing pop animation and a single success haptic. Locked milestones show the requirement, never a padlock. P2.

23 · Daily challenge

Aurel does not need a separate daily challenge — the arc is the ritual (R1), and adding a third daily obligation would dilute it. The one exception worth testing: on days when the arc is already complete, offer a single optional Sundown extra — one mixed item, from anything the learner has ever seen, framed as "one more, for the pleasure of it". No reward, no streak effect. P2, A/B tested.

24 · Personalization

Onboarding already collects goal (up to two) and level (with a real six-question placement). Two additions and one visible payoff:

Ask the daily commitment — 5, 10, or 20 minutes, phrased in the brand's voice ("How much of a morning can you spare?"). It sets the arc's size, the reminder time, and the lesson length. Currently unasked, which is why no daily unit exists. P0.

Ask nothing else. Interests can be inferred from the goal; a fourth question costs more activation than it returns.

Show the personalization. After placement, a plan screen that names what was chosen and why: "B1, work-first. Twelve chapters ahead of you. Meetings and email come in chapter three — we've moved it up." One screen, and it is the moment the app proves it listened. P0.

25 · Onboarding

Current sequence: Welcome → Goal → Level → Placement (6 questions, editable, confirm) → Home. It is well-paced, it never feels like a survey, and the placement-review pattern is exemplary.

Target sequence: Welcome → Goal → Level → Commitment → Placement → Confirm → Your plan → First tiny lesson (3 items) → First arc half-complete → Home, with the sun already at the meridian.

The critical change is the last three steps: today the learner reaches the home screen having learned nothing. A three-item first lesson before Home means the first session delivers a real English win, a first reward, and a personalised roadmap — the specification's activation requirement — in under three minutes.

Account creation stays where it is — after value, not before. The welcome screen's "I have an account" link is the correct and only pre-value account affordance. P0 for the first-lesson insertion.

26 · Notifications

Settings currently offers one daily reminder at 19:30 and a weekly email. Keep the restraint; add granularity and honesty of category — the learner should be able to turn off competition pings and keep review ones.

27 · Visual identity

Direction to hold: unhurried dawn — warm, editorial, quiet, adult. It is working. The discipline needed now is subtractive, and there is one systemic error to correct.

Recommendation R5 · Glass is for controls, not for content

What: The 20px-blur glass recipe is currently applied to nearly every surface: option rows, answer tiles, word chips, story cards, stat panels, settings groups, the lesson footer, the tab bar, and — until recently — the primary button itself. Restrict it to the functional layer: tab bar, sticky lesson footer, and modal sheets. Content surfaces become plain tinted surface fills with a hairline edge and one soft shadow — the same shapes and spacing, less machinery.

Why: everything translucent means nothing reads as elevated, and answer text sits on a moving background. · UX: restores figure-ground; options stop competing with the ambient layer behind them. · Learning: higher text contrast on exactly the surfaces the learner must read. · Retention: reduces the low-grade visual fatigue of long sessions. · Visual: makes the remaining glass mean something. · Complexity: low — one shared style value. · Priority: P0.

Ambient layer: keep the drifting fields and contour, but only on the four screens where they earn attention — Welcome, Home, Result, Paywall. On lesson and list screens they add motion behind text for no benefit. Halve their opacity where they stay.

The mark: the A-with-horizon wordmark is good and small-size legible. Lock the sun disc position and crossbar weight now, and derive the app icon from the disc-and-crossbar alone — the full letter will not survive at 60px.

28 · Colour system

The palette is right; the semantics are underspecified. Assign every role explicitly, and note that terracotta is currently doing three jobs at once — brand, primary action, and error — which is the one thing a semantic system must not allow.

29 · Typography

Two families, both already in place: Caprasimo for display and headings, Figtree for everything else. Keep. Codify the scale that the build uses inconsistently (17px, 15.5px, 14.5px, 13.5px, 12.5px all appear within one screen):

Display 44/1.02 Caprasimo · Title 31/1.1 · Screen title 29/1.12 · Section 25/1.15 · Card title 17/1.2 Caprasimo · Body 15/1.55 Figtree · Secondary 13.5/1.5 · Caption 12/1.45 · Kicker 10.5 700 0.16em uppercase · Numeric Figtree 700 tabular

Nine sizes, no exceptions, and never below 12px. Numerals are always tabular — the build already does this and it shows.

Dynamic Type is the gap. The Settings text-size slider affects one preview line, not the app. Everything must scale with the system size, with layouts tested at the largest accessibility size: the 5-node path, the option rows, and the lesson footer are the three places that will break first. Kickers and tab labels should scale but may cap. P1.

30 · Iconography

The current set is hand-drawn, 2.75px stroke, rounded caps — consistent and appropriate. In production, use SF Symbols for anything with a system meaning (chevrons, close, lock, gear, microphone, speaker, play) and keep custom drawings only for the brand-carrying marks: the wordmark, the sun, the dune horizon, and the mastery ring. One stroke weight, one corner treatment, 24pt grid, always paired with a label in navigation. Never an icon alone as the sole meaning of a state.

31 · Component library

The build already has a de facto system. Formalise it as thirteen components with the geometry it already uses, so the next twenty screens cost a fraction of the first eighteen.

Spacing 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 20 · 24 · 32 · 44 (screen padding is 24, section gap 22–26, list gap 10–12). Radii 12 chip · 16 tile · 20 button · 24 row · 28 card · 999 pill. Elevation three levels only: flat, resting (one soft shadow), raised (contact + ambient).

Components: Button (primary / ghost / link / destructive) · Option row · Answer tile · Word chip · Content card · Stat panel · Path node · Skill meter · Mastery ring · Progress bar (linear, segmented, ring) · Verdict banner · Sheet · Tab bar. Each needs the twelve states in §32 below drawn before hand-off — the current build has default, selected, and correct/incorrect only.

32 · Component states

Required for every interactive component: default · pressed · focused (visible ring for keyboard and Switch Control) · selected · disabled · loading · success · error · empty · completed · locked · partially completed. Three are missing across the entire build and are the most common cause of a prototype feeling unfinished: focused, loading, and partially completed. Add them to the primary button, option row, and path node first. P1.

33 · Motion system

The motion language is already the most finished system in the product. Document it as-is and stop adding: screen entry 440ms rise-and-settle · staggered group entry 60ms steps · card flip 680ms with perspective · correct answer pop 340ms · wrong answer shake 380ms · count-up 820ms cubic ease-out · node breathing 3.8s · ambient drift 24–33s.

Two things to remove: the sheen sweep on any surface that carries a label (it moves light across text — the button already lost it, the path node and the mic have not), and the ambient drift on lesson screens (§27).

One to add: the arc's sun movement, 900ms, ease-out, on returning to Home after each act — the single most important animation in the redesign, because it is the reward. Under Reduce Motion the sun crossfades between three positions instead of travelling. P0 with R1.

34 · Haptics

Map, and use nothing outside the map: selection change → light selection · correct → success (light) · not-quite → warning (soft, never error-sharp) · item mastered → success (medium) · arc completed → the one distinctive sequence in the app: two light taps and a soft settle, matched to the sun setting · milestone → the same sequence, once. No haptic on scroll, tab change, ordinary buttons, or card flips. The existing Settings toggle stays and must genuinely disable all of it. P1.

35 · Sound design

Nothing is specified today beyond a Settings toggle labelled "Soft and sparse" — which is exactly the right brief. Eight sounds, one instrument family (struck wood and low strings, no synth, no arcade): lesson start, correct, not-quite, item complete, arc complete, milestone, recording start, recording stop. All under 400ms except arc complete (1.2s). Off by default on first launch; the toggle already exists. Never a sound for navigation. P2.

36 · Accessibility

Partially handled — progress bars carry roles and values, buttons have labels, reduced motion is honoured, touch targets are 44px. The gaps, in priority order:

1. Dynamic Type is not wired to the app (§29). P1.

2. Path nodes have no accessible label. Each needs "Lesson 2, Introductions, locked — complete Greetings to open", not an unlabelled circle. P0.

3. Matched pairs and correct answers rely on colour plus opacity. Add the glyph (§28). P0.

4. No focus states for keyboard or Switch Control. P1.

5. Audio-only items need a text alternative — transcript after answering, always; and a non-speaking path through every speaking item. P0.

6. Announce verdicts with an accessibility notification so VoiceOver users hear the result without hunting for it. P1.

7. Contrast audit of the 45% text mixes on the ambient background, and of white labels on accent-600 fills, at both themes. Two of the three currently sit near the boundary. P1.

37 · Dark mode

Fully implemented with its own accent lift and edge/highlight values — genuinely good. Two corrections: the sage on dark backgrounds is the weakest colour pair in the product and needs a lighter step for text use; and the ambient glow is still slightly hot on the darkest surfaces at 34% — take it to 24% and let the contour carry the depth instead. Verify every state pair (correct, not-quite, locked, mastered) in dark before hand-off; today only the common ones have been checked. P1.

38 · Performance

Four watch-points, all created by the visual system: the ambient blur fields and contour are full-screen composited layers and should be static images on the screens that keep them, not live blurs; the glass surfaces multiply blur passes when stacked (R5 removes most of them); audio must be pre-buffered before a listening item renders, or the item shows its loading state; and speech recognition should run on-device where available so the flow survives offline. Budget: first meaningful screen under 400ms, lesson start under 200ms, answer feedback under 60ms — that last one is what makes an app feel expensive.

39 · Offline and interruption

Nothing exists today; design these five before hand-off. Interrupted lesson — resume prompt naming the question ("Pick up question 3 of 5?") with a discard option. Offline — the current chapter's lessons and audio are downloaded; the banner reads "Working offline — today's lesson is here" rather than an error. Audio unavailable — per-item retry plus skip, never a silent dead item. Mic denied — explanation plus Settings link plus a typed alternative so the item is still completable. Sync failure — the arc completes locally and reconciles later; never lose a completed day to a network error. P1, all five.

40 · Analytics

Instrument before shipping the redesign, or none of the changes can be judged. Minimum event set, grouped by the question it answers:

Activation: onboarding step completed (per step), placement completed, plan screen seen, first lesson completed, first arc completed. Engagement: arcs completed per week, act-2 completion rate given act 1, items per session, session length, scene started/finished, speaking attempts per item. Retention: D1 / D7 / D30, weeks with ≥4 arcs, absence length before return. Learning: first-attempt accuracy by skill, correct-on-retry rate (the R4 metric), review success by interval, items reaching Strong and Mastered, speaking verdict distribution and recogniser confidence. UX: drop-off by screen and by question index, retry rate, time to first interaction, replay count on listening items.

The north-star metric is weekly arcs completed, not session length. Explicitly do not optimise for time in app; a shorter session with higher review success is a better product.

41 · A/B opportunities

Six, in order of expected information: 1 arc module on Home versus streak pill (primary test of R1) · 2 commitment question in onboarding versus a 10-minute default · 3 second-attempt path versus immediate reveal (R4) · 4 five skill meters versus the minutes chart first (R3) · 5 Sundown notification on versus off by default · 6 Sundown extra offered versus not (§23). Measure each against weekly arcs and review success, never against session length. Do not test the visual identity; preference is not evidence.

42 · Signature innovation

The one thing nobody else does

The horizon day: a lesson at dawn, sixty seconds at sundown.

Every other app in the category asks for one visit and hides its review scheduler inside it. Aurel splits the day in two and puts the scheduler on the surface as the product's central image. The sun crosses the horizon only as real learning happens: it rises with the lesson, and it sets when the words that were about to fade have been caught. The arc is the streak, the progress bar, and the day's contract, in one drawing that is unmistakably this app's.

Why it holds up: it is honest (the sun cannot be moved by scrolling), it is short (six minutes plus sixty seconds), it is on-brand ("English, unhurried"), it makes spaced repetition — the app's real educational claim — into the thing the learner looks forward to, and it is cheap to build on top of the scheduler that already exists. Second return per day is the retention mechanic; catching a word before it fades is the emotional one.

Not recommended for MVP: camera-based vocabulary capture. It is a strong mechanic but an Apple Design Award winner already occupies that ground, and it sits awkwardly beside a curriculum built on a CEFR ladder. Keep it as a P3 experiment, scoped to the goal the learner picked ("Find three words in your office"), and only after the horizon day is proven.

Purpose / goal / CTAs. Make speaking English feel safe and improvable. Primary: the microphone. Secondary: "Hear it again", "Play mine", "Try again", "Skip".

45 · MVP, phase 2, phase 3

MVP — the honest daily product. All of P0. The product this produces: a personalised plan, a defined day with two acts, a lesson engine with retry and eight item types, a real speaking loop, honest per-skill progress, and one visual signature. That is a shippable premium app.

46 · Risks and trade-offs

Two acts a day may be one too many. The arc asks for a second return. If act-2 completion sits below 40% after the A/B test, make Sundown optional decoration on the arc rather than a requirement for the streak — but do not remove the review session; move it into the lesson's tail instead.

Speech recognition will disappoint some accents. Mitigated by never showing a number, always allowing skip, and saying "I couldn't hear that well enough" instead of "wrong". This is a content and copy risk more than a technical one.

Mastery-requires-production makes progress slower. Intentional, and the honest choice — but it means the numbers on Progress will move less than competitors'. Compensate with capability statements, which move on chapter completion.

Removing glass may feel like a downgrade. It will look plainer in a side-by-side screenshot and better in a five-minute session. Judge it on the second.

Scenes are content-expensive. Twelve scenes per level is real writing and voice work. Ship four per level at first, tied to the most-chosen goal.

A quiet product converts more slowly. No urgency, no scarcity, one paywall trigger. Accept a lower day-one conversion for a materially better retention curve, and watch trial-to-paid rather than trial-start rate.

47 · Final design-quality checklist

Run this against every screen before hand-off. A screen that fails any line is not finished.

Educational — The learning objective is nameable in one sentence. · The learner produces something, not only recognises. · A mistake teaches before it resolves. · Nothing on screen implies progress that did not happen.

UX — Exactly one dominant action. · The button label says what will happen. · Back always exists and always looks the same. · Nothing is more than two taps from the day's next act.

Engagement — Every reward names an achievement, not a quantity. · There is a defined end to the day. · No shame in any failure state. · Competition can be switched off entirely.

Visual — Sizes come from the nine-step scale. · Spacing comes from the eight-step scale. · Glass appears only on control layers. · Two accent colours maximum per screen. · The screen is recognisably Aurel with the wordmark cropped out.

iOS — System components where system behaviour is expected. · Targets ≥44pt. · Dynamic Type to the largest accessibility size. · Reduce Motion has a real alternative, not just a disabled animation. · Nothing requires a gesture that has no visible affordance.

Premium — Answer feedback under 60ms. · Every animation names a cause. · Empty, loading, offline, and error states are drawn, not assumed. · One haptic per meaningful outcome, never per tap. · Nothing on the screen is there because the space looked bare.

Needs product clarification

1. Speech recognition confidence. Which engine, and what confidence figures does it return in practice for non-native speech? The entire speaking verdict design depends on a real threshold. Until this is known, the three-verdict model is a design intention, not a specification.

2. Audio assets. Is native audio recorded per item, per sentence, or synthesised? Listening, scenes, stories, and the speaking loop all assume real audio exists.

3. Free versus Pro boundary. The build locks chapter two onward and two of three stories, but the rule is not stated anywhere. What exactly is free — a chapter, a week, a daily allowance?

4. Cohort mechanics. Is Cedar Group real, matched, or seeded? What happens at week's end, and does anything carry over? Also: the current rows show city names — is that consented data?

5. Review scheduler. Which algorithm, and what intervals? The copy promises "a widening interval"; the design shows "due tomorrow / 2 days / 4 days". These must match the real scheduler.

6. Curriculum size. The paywall claims twenty-four chapters A1–C1. How many exist as content today? Locked-state copy and the ladder both depend on the real number.

7. Stories. Is the reader in scope? The list currently navigates nowhere.

8. Localisation. Is a right-to-left or Arabic UI planned? It changes the arc drawing, the path geometry, and the typography stack, and is far cheaper to accommodate now than later.

If only three things get built

1. The arc — give the day a shape and an end. 2. The speaking loop — hear, say, compare, again. 3. Five skill meters — show ability, not attendance. Everything else in this document is refinement; these three change what the product is.
