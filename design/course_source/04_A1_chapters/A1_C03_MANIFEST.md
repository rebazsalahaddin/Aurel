## Mission and can-dos

**Mission:** meet two recurring characters, share country/language information, state a job/role, and introduce another person.

**Can-dos:** ask/answer origin · state language(s) · state role/job · introduce another person · understand short identity profiles.

**Country-record ruling (documented for lens 3):** each of the 10 country–nationality–language triples gets ONE vocab record (headword = country name; nationality adjective and language(s) stored in-record). Productive target = the country name inside `I'm from …`; nationality adjectives and language names are **recognition-level** (recognize heard/seen; repeat within the modelled `They speak …` frame). §9.3 does not add nationality adjectives as productive A1 grammar. Ten countries across six continents (NA×2, SA×2, Africa×2, Asia×2, Europe×1, Oceania×1); ≥4 continents required ✓. Every record carries a creator note that the country has more languages/identities than shown — never imply one language or one identity.

**Jobs ruling:** the nine-job list is fixed (§9.3). Set E is 4 items, below the 5–8 floor — same attached-set disposition as C2-B/C2-D (attached to Set D's construct in the same teach screen; full encounter count still met via cast anchoring).

**Do not teach (this chapter):** `shop assistant` (Sam's job — he asks/answers only with taught items) · `Where do you live?` · nationality adjectives as productive grammar · the `a/an` rule before L2 (L1 presents `I'm a/an …` as a fixed frame only) · `he/she/they` as analysed pronouns before L2 (L1 uses 3rd-person forms **only inside fixed chunks** like `Where is Alex from?`) · countries/languages beyond the roster · flags as any semantic cue (art shows highlighted map regions, never flags) · immigration/status questions of any kind · present simple beyond the two lexical frames (`I/They speak …`, `What do you do?` — full paradigm is C7).
